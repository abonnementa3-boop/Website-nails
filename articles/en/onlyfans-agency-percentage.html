In shortAn OnlyFans agency usually keeps between 30% and 60% of revenue, after the 20% OnlyFans takes. The rate isn't what matters: what matters is what you keep at the end of the month compared with going solo.

The splits you'll come across

Agency shareWhat it usually covers
20 to 30%Chatting only, often on limited hours. Socials and traffic stay with you.
40 to 50%Full chatting, social media management, pricing strategy.
50 to 60% and upAll of the above plus traffic generation, or launching an account from scratch.

These are orders of magnitude, not rules. Each agency sets its own terms.

First, the OnlyFans fee

OnlyFans keeps 20% of everything you earn: subscriptions, paid content, tips. You receive 80% of what fans pay. The split with the agency comes next, and the contract should say on which base: almost always on that 80%.

A worked example

Say your fans pay $10,000 in a month.

  1. OnlyFans keeps 20%: $8,000 left.
  2. On a 50/50 split, the agency gets $4,000 and you keep $4,000.

On your own you'd have kept the $8,000. So the question is whether the agency more than doubles what your fans spend. If sales go from $10,000 to $30,000, you keep $12,000 instead of $8,000, while working less. If they stay at $10,000, you've lost half your income.

Why the lowest rate isn't the best deal

An agency at 30% that only answers messages leaves you everything else: posting daily, bringing all the traffic. An agency at 50% that also runs your socials and generates traffic on top of yours works all three levers at once.

New creators

Launching from zero means more work for the agency and no revenue at first, so a new creator's share is often lower at the start. What matters is that the contract allows it to be renegotiated once the account takes off. That's how it works at Naylis.

What to get in writing

  • The percentage and the base it applies to.
  • Who pays any costs (ads, tools).
  • When and how the agency's share is settled.
  • Renegotiation and exit conditions.
