In shortThree rules prevent almost every problem: never pay to join an agency, stay the holder of your account and of the email attached to it, and don't start anything without a written contract that says how you leave.

Why there are so many fake agencies

Starting an "OnlyFans agency" takes an Instagram account and a logo. Some people use that to collect money, content or accounts. Others aren't dishonest, just unable to deliver: they take a percentage and add nothing. Either way, you lose.

The 10 red flags

1. You're asked to pay to start

Sign-up fee, deposit, mandatory training, "starter pack": an agency earns from your revenue, not before it.

2. You're promised a precise income

"$10k in your first month, guaranteed": nobody can guarantee what your fans will spend.

3. They want to change the email or bank account on your profile

Both must stay yours. That's what lets you take the account back, and it's where payouts must land.

4. No contract, or pressure to sign fast

A contract is read calmly. If you're not given time, leave.

5. No exit clause

Multi-year lock-ins, huge penalties, rights over your content after the end: clauses built to trap you.

6. They ask for content before any agreement

An application doesn't need intimate photos. Never send them to someone you haven't signed with.

7. You can't tell who is behind it

No website, no names, only an anonymous social account.

8. Mass outreach

The same copy-pasted DM sent to hundreds of creators is not recruiting.

9. No precise answers

Who chats? On which hours? Where does traffic come from? Vague answers usually hide a missing team.

10. Pressure to go beyond your limits

Your limits on content are yours. An agency that pushes them isn't working for you.

What a serious agency does instead

  • Asks about your situation before making an offer.
  • Puts the percentage, the team and the launch plan in writing first.
  • Leaves you as the account holder.
  • Tells you how to leave.

If you have a doubt

  1. Sign nothing and send nothing until it's cleared up.
  2. Ask to talk to a creator already working with the agency.
  3. Have the contract read by someone independent.
  4. If you already gave access, change your password and turn on two-factor authentication.
