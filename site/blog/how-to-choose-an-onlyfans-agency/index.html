How to choose an OnlyFans agency: 9 things to check.

Updated November 23, 2026 · 2 min read

In shortDon't compare income promises: compare actual work. Who chats and at what hours, who runs your socials, where the traffic comes from, how many creators each team handles, and what the contract says about leaving.

Where to look for an agency

Creators find agencies three ways: search engines, social media (many recruit on Instagram and X) and word of mouth. The third is the most reliable: a creator who has worked with an agency for a year will tell you in five minutes what no website will.

Whatever the route, be wary of unsolicited messages announcing a guaranteed income. A serious agency asks you questions before it makes promises.

The 9 criteria

1. Who does the chatting, and when

Ask which hours are covered. "24/7" should mean teams taking shifts, not one chatter replying when he can. Ask how chatters are trained and whether they follow written sales scripts.

2. Who runs your social media

If the answer is "you", the agency is doing half the job.

3. Where the traffic comes from

This is the question that separates agencies. Some wait for you to bring every subscriber. Others generate traffic too. Ask what, concretely, the agency does to bring people to your page.

4. How many creators per team

A manager following forty accounts can't know yours. Ask whether you get a dedicated team and who your contact is.

5. The percentage, and what it's calculated on

A percentage alone means nothing. You need to know whether it applies before or after the OnlyFans fee, and what it covers. See OnlyFans agency percentages.

6. The contract

It must exist, be written, and state the term, the percentage, the exit conditions and what happens to your account and content at the end.

7. Access to your account

You stay the account holder, keep the email attached to it and can change the password at any time.

8. The numbers you're shown

A serious agency gives you access to your statistics and explains its decisions.

9. How they talk to you

Pressure to sign quickly, vague terms, income figures quoted before seeing your account: reasons to leave.

Red flags

  • You're asked to pay to start. Sign-up fees, deposits, mandatory paid training: an agency earns when you earn.
  • They want to change the email or bank details on your account. Payouts must land in your own bank account.
  • No exit clause, or a multi-year lock-in with heavy penalties.
  • They ask for content before any agreement.
  • You can't tell who is behind it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if an OnlyFans agency is legit?

It answers your questions precisely, offers a written contract with an exit clause, leaves you as the account holder and doesn't promise an income before studying your situation.

Should I pay to join an agency?

No. An agency is paid through a percentage of your revenue. Entry fees or mandatory paid training are red flags.

Can an agency reject my application?

Yes. A serious agency reviews each profile and only takes accounts it believes it can grow.
